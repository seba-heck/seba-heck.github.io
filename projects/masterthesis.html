Abstract

This thesis presents the development of large-scale electromagnetic simulation datasets for training machine learning models. Benchmark problems from the T.E.A.M. (Testing Electromagnetic Analysis Methods) series and additional electromagnetic scattering problems are implemented using finite element methods within the Netgen/NGSolve framework. The considered problems include asymmetric eddy-current simulations, nonlinear magnetostatic systems, transient rotational electromagnetic devices, and three-dimensional wave scattering scenarios. For each problem, physically relevant parameters such as conductivity, excitation frequency, magnetisation curves, and geometric configurations are systematically varied to generate diverse simulation samples. The method ensures physical diversity and numerical consistency while maintaining computational efficiency through controlled and tested sampling methods. The resulting datasets comprise thousands of three-dimensional samples covering a broad range of electromagnetic phenomena and material behaviours, providing a foundation for future research on data-driven methods like neural operators or foundation models.
